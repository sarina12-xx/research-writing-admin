## History

On November 20 2025, Rick Scott introduced My More Affordable Care Act: brings power back to Americans instead of funneling tax dollars to insurance companies Funds are applied to premiums and continue to exist as Hyde-compliant by restricting use on abortion health plans or abortion services  The bill plans to use HSA-style Trump Health Freedom Accounts to put health care decisions in the hands of Americans

## CBO analysis


CBO responded to Scott's proposal, believing that the proposed health care plan would increase the statistic of uninsured category by 14 million people next year. Ultimately, this would culminate  from people choosing not to buy insurance after tax penalties for those without coverage are repealed.  Governor Rick Scott hasn't responded to the CBO analysis where they estimate health insurance premiums would be 15-20% higher than it is under current law. By 2026, CBO predicted that with the proposal, 52 million will be uninsured if the proposal took place. Now in 2026, according to the Common Wealth Fund Survey, the survey claims that 92 million working age adults are uninsured, which is 46% of the adult population. The estimate is off by 40 million.

The proposal claims that under current insurance law, in 2026 as predicted in 2017, a single 21 year old earning $26,500 with insurance policy would cost $5100 a year and would get tax credit of $3400 with them paying $1700 of the premium. In addition, under republican bill, person’s share of cost would drop to $1450

However, this act would repeal cost sharing subsidies for low-income people, resulting in higher deductibles and out of pocket costs. This also means removing income based tax credits that help people pay for coverage, and ending penalties for people who don’t have health insurance and phase out medicaid.
